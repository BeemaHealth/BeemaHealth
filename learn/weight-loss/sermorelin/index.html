Sermorelin is not an FDA-approved adult weight-loss GLP-1

Geref's actual job, and why it left the market

Sermorelin is a 29-amino-acid analog of the N-terminal sequence of human growth hormone-releasing hormone. Under the brand Geref it was FDA-approved for diagnosing growth-hormone deficiency and for treating idiopathic GH deficiency in children with growth failure. In 2008, EMD Serono discontinued the products. FDA withdrew the NDAs in 2009. A 2013 Federal Register notice later confirmed the discontinuation was not because the agency found the products unsafe or ineffective. Commercial demand and manufacturing, not a toxicity scandal, ended branded supply.

This page is educational, not medical advice. Beema Health does not offer sermorelin today and does not advertise it as a fat-loss peptide.

Why adult fat-loss marketing overreaches

Growth hormone affects body composition, which is why wellness clinics retell sermorelin as a lean-mass shot. That leap skips the evidence standard used for modern obesity drugs: large randomized trials with percent weight change, cardiometabolic endpoints, and labeled risk language. Sermorelin never had a Wegovy-style obesity indication. Hype for unapproved fat-loss use is exactly what this article will not repeat as a benefit claim.

If an adult has documented growth-hormone deficiency, recombinant growth hormone - not a discontinued diagnostic peptide - is the usual labeled therapy. That diagnosis is specialized. Beema Health's weight-loss intake is not an endocrine stimulation-test clinic.

Sermorelin versus GLP-1-class medicines

GLP-1 receptor agonists reduce energy intake through gut-brain signaling. Sermorelin tries to raise pituitary growth-hormone pulses. Side-effect profiles differ. Glucose, edema, carpal-tunnel symptoms, and IGF-1 excess are growth-hormone-axis concerns. Nausea and delayed gastric emptying are incretin-axis concerns. Mixing the two in a 'peptide stack' without a clinician is unsafe amateur pharmacology.

  • Sermorelin: GHRH analog, historic pediatric GH use, no current FDA-approved U.S. brand
  • Tesamorelin: related axis, but a different molecule with a current HIV lipodystrophy label
  • Semaglutide/tirzepatide: incretin agonists with obesity-trial evidence Beema Health may evaluate as compounded options

Compounded and research-chemical sermorelin

After Geref left the market, compounding pharmacies and internet vendors filled the gap. Compounded sermorelin is not FDA-approved as a finished drug. Research-labeled vials are not medicines. Beema Health will not sell either. Prescription status does not disappear because a website uses the word peptide.

Athletes should also know that growth-hormone releasing factors are prohibited in sport under WADA rules. That is another reason this is not a casual wellness add-on.

Safety issues this page will not minimize

Stimulating growth hormone can raise IGF-1. People with active malignancy, uncontrolled diabetes, or untreated pituitary disease need specialist judgment before any GH-axis drug. Injection-site reactions occur. Long-term adult wellness dosing has a thin evidence file compared with incretin obesity trials. Absence of a 2008 safety withdrawal is not proof that off-label adult fat-loss use is wise.

Frequently asked questions

Was sermorelin ever FDA-approved?
Yes, historically. Geref (sermorelin acetate) was approved for diagnostic use and for treating idiopathic growth hormone deficiency in children with growth failure. The manufacturer discontinued the branded products in 2008. FDA later determined the withdrawal was not for safety or effectiveness reasons. There is no currently marketed FDA-approved sermorelin product for adult weight loss.
Does Beema Health prescribe sermorelin?
No. Beema Health does not offer sermorelin, Geref, or anti-aging growth-hormone protocols. Live evaluation is for compounded semaglutide or compounded tirzepatide when a licensed provider finds them appropriate and legally available. Compounded semaglutide is not FDA-approved and is considered only when legally available and clinically appropriate. Compounded tirzepatide is not FDA-approved and is considered only when legally available and clinically appropriate.
Is sermorelin a GLP-1 peptide for weight loss?
No. Sermorelin is a fragment analog of growth hormone-releasing hormone. It is not a GLP-1 receptor agonist. Ads that bundle sermorelin with retatrutide or semaglutide as 'weight-loss peptides' mix unrelated hormone systems. GLP-1 medicines used for weight management are prescription medications, not research chemicals.
Do compounded sermorelin vials equal Geref?
No. Compounded preparations are not the discontinued branded product and have not been shown in modern obesity trials to match GLP-1 outcomes. Compounding a GHRH analog for adult fat loss is a different legal and clinical question from Beema Health's compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide program. Beema Health does not provide sermorelin compounding.
Is there strong evidence sermorelin causes meaningful fat loss?
The original approvals were about pediatric growth-hormone deficiency and diagnostic testing, not STEP-style percent body-weight endpoints. Adult 'wellness' use is off-label with limited high-quality obesity data. This article will not hype unapproved fat-loss claims. A licensed endocrinologist - not a peptide catalog - should evaluate true growth-hormone disorders.
What should I use this page for?
Use it to avoid a category error. If you want medical weight-loss care, read GLP-1 education and Beema Health's live program pages. If you were told you have adult growth-hormone deficiency, that workup belongs in specialty care with IGF-1 testing and stimulation tests, not in a GLP-1 telehealth intake.
