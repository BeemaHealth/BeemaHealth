# Certification Language and Seal Usage Rules

Primary source: [Healthcare Certification Terms and Conditions](https://www.legitscript.com/certification/healthcare-certification/terms-and-conditions/) (fetched 2026-08-16). Quotes are as extracted from that page; treat as close paraphrase, not a guaranteed verbatim legal quote — pull the live terms before anything contractual or legally load-bearing.

## Approved language for describing certification status

The terms explicitly permit exactly one described pattern:

> "Applicant is allowed to advertise itself as a 'LegitScript-certified website.'"

The terms do not enumerate a longer list of pre-approved marketing phrases. LegitScript's FAQ pages reference "media guidelines" for more detailed display requirements, described as "available upon request" — meaning the fuller style guide is not fully public. If Beema needs precise wording beyond "LegitScript-certified website" (e.g. for a hero section or a badge caption), request LegitScript's media guidelines directly rather than improvising language and assuming it's covered.

## Seal display rules

- **Placement:** the Seal may be displayed "on Applicant's home page" — the terms describe home-page placement, not a general license to place it anywhere on the site. Confirm with LegitScript whether other pages (e.g. a treatment page or pricing page) are also permitted before placing it there.
- **Must be dynamic, not static:** "use of the Seal in a static form is not permitted." The Seal is described as an "HTML-based or JAVA based, trademarked...symbol" that must "automatically refresh images of the Seal in a timely manner." A plain screenshotted or hard-coded static image of the seal is explicitly against the terms — it has to be the live, refreshing embed.
- **Hyperlink requirement:** the Seal "will only hyperlink to a page within legitscript.com identified by LegitScript" — i.e., clicking it must go to LegitScript's own verification page for the certified entity, not somewhere else.
- **No modification:** "agrees not to modify the Approval in any form including its size."
- **No use on other websites:** "agrees not to permit the use of the Approval on any website other than a website explicitly authorized by LegitScript." This is the basis for the "seal goes on the certified website only" rule in SKILL.md — it also means the seal cannot be embedded on a *different* Beema-controlled property (a landing page on a separate domain, a partner microsite) unless that specific domain is itself the authorized/certified one.

**Not confirmed in the terms and conditions text pulled:** whether the seal may appear in ad creative, printed materials, decks, email, or social posts. The terms are silent on those surfaces specifically — the safest reading, and the one SKILL.md currently states, is to keep the seal to the certified website only and treat any other placement as unconfirmed until LegitScript's media guidelines are obtained directly.

## Endorsement, sponsorship, and quality implications

> "display of the Approval does not imply sponsorship or endorsement by LegitScript of Applicant's products or services" and "will not be used or displayed in a manner suggesting such sponsorship or endorsement."

> Applicant agrees "not to use or display the Approval in any manner that is misleading, defamatory...or otherwise objectionable."

**Not explicitly found in the terms text pulled:** a standalone clause specifically naming "you may not imply a competitor's lack of certification makes them unethical or subpar." That framing in SKILL.md is a reasonable inference from the broader "not misleading, defamatory, or disparaging" language rather than a directly quoted clause — flag it as inference, not verbatim rule, until the fuller terms or media guidelines confirm explicit competitor-disparagement language.

## Affiliate disclosure and linking restriction

> Applicant "will fully disclose affiliates in the application," where "Affiliates" is defined as "any individual, business, or entity who previously, currently, or is expected to have a commercial and/or professional relationship."

> Applicant agrees "it will not link or otherwise refer Internet users to healthcare merchant or advertiser websites that are not approved by LegitScript."

**Beema/Bask implication:** this is a hard linking restriction, not just a disclosure requirement. If Bask's domain is not itself LegitScript-approved, this clause reads as prohibiting Beema's certified site from linking/CTA'ing into it — independent of whatever Standard 4 (Affiliates and Partners) requires. This is a second, separate reason (beyond Standard 4) that Bask's certification status needs written confirmation before Beema's own certification claims or seal usage go live.

## Public Verification Link vs. certified website URL

Not defined in the Terms and Conditions document itself. Per LegitScript's public materials on platform registration (search results, fetched 2026-08-16, not a single authoritative page — re-verify): the **Public Verification Link** is the link used to *register* certification status with third-party platforms (Google's healthcare certification form, LinkedIn, TikTok, Netflix ad platforms are cited examples) and to let the public verify certification status. It is distinct from the **certified website URL** itself — Meta's Prescription Drug Ads authorization form specifically asks for the certified website URL, not the verification link (see [[meta-ads-compliance]]). Confirm the exact distinction and current examples of the Public Verification Link format directly from LegitScript's help center before filing any platform application — this section is the least directly sourced part of this file.

## Lapse and revocation

> "Applicant may only use the Approval as long as Applicant remains certified by LegitScript."

> "LegitScript reserves the right to terminate or modify Applicant's license to use the Approval at any time for any reason."

> Applicant "is responsible for canceling certification prior to renewal date to avoid paying applicable annual fees."

**Practical implication:** the moment certification lapses or is revoked, continued display of the seal or continued "LegitScript-certified" language becomes a terms violation, not just a stale claim. This also means Meta's authorization (which requires *active* certification) and Google's healthcare certification are both immediately at risk the moment LegitScript status lapses — renewal should be tracked as an ad-account availability dependency, not a back-office administrative task. See [[google-ads-compliance]], [[meta-ads-compliance]].
