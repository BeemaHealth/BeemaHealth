# Why GLP-1 medications reduce hunger, and how the three types differ

## Facts and sources
- GLP-1 is an incretin hormone released by intestinal L cells after eating. Its natural half-life is about 2 minutes because DPP-4 degrades it. (Drucker DJ, Cell Metab 2018;27:740-56; Müller TD et al., Mol Metab 2019;30:72-130.)
- GLP-1 receptor agonists act on the hypothalamus (activating satiety POMC/CART neurons, inhibiting hunger AgRP/NPY neurons), brainstem, and reward circuits, and slow gastric emptying. (Secher A et al., J Clin Invest 2014;124:4473-88; Gabery S et al., JCI Insight 2020;5:e133429; van Bloemendaal L et al., Diabetes 2014;63:4186-96 for reward.)
- Common side effects are gastrointestinal (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea), tied to slowed gastric emptying and central effects.
- Single GLP-1 agonist: activates GLP-1 receptor only. Dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist: adds the GIP receptor; preclinical work suggests CNS GIPR signaling reduces food intake and may reduce nausea. (Zhang Q et al., Cell Metab 2021;33:833-44; Borner T et al., Cell Metab 2021.) Wording stays "may".
- Triple GIP/GLP-1/glucagon agonist: adds the glucagon receptor, which may raise energy expenditure and hepatic fat oxidation. Investigational, not FDA-approved. (Coskun T et al., Cell Metab 2022;34:1234-47; Jastreboff AM et al., NEJM 2023;389:514-26.)
- Weekly dosing reflects half-lives of roughly 5 to 7 days for the long-acting molecules.

## Narration
1. hook: Hunger isn't just willpower. It's a conversation between your gut and your brain, and GLP-1 medications change that conversation.
2. gut: After you eat, special cells in your gut release a hormone called GLP-1. It travels through the blood to tell your brain: you're full.
3. brain: In the hypothalamus, the brain's appetite center, GLP-1 switches on fullness neurons and quiets hunger neurons. It also turns down the reward signals behind cravings.
4. stomach: It also slows how quickly the stomach empties, so you feel full for longer. That's also why nausea is a common side effect.
5. lasting: The catch: natural GLP-1 is broken down within minutes. These medications are engineered to last for days, so the fullness signal stays on.
6. type1: There are three types. The first, a single agonist, activates only the GLP-1 receptor.
7. type2: The second, a dual agonist, also activates the receptor for a second gut hormone called GIP, which may add appetite effects and help tolerability.
8. type3: The third, a triple agonist, adds the glucagon receptor, which may increase energy use. This type is research only right now. It is not approved for use.
9. beema: Beema Health does not sell the third type. Beema Health offers only compounded medications in the first two categories. Compounded medications are not FDA-approved, and a licensed provider decides whether any treatment is appropriate.
